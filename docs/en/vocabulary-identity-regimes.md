# Identity Regime Vocabulary

An identity regime specifies criteria governing identity judgments and the
conditions under which those criteria apply.

## Foundational Questions

- **Individuation**: what establishes one whole under a specified criterion,
  and what distinguishes it from other wholes?
- **Co-reference**: when do different references concern the same referent?
- **Persistence**: what licenses stages across change as stages of the same
  continuing individual?

Justifying an identity criterion for a regime draws on these questions.

## Transformation Behavior

The classification of a transformation depends on its admissible effects
and the identity criterion against which those effects are evaluated.
Field granularity and explicit transformation warrants may affect the
resulting classification.

Transformation theory defines a separate operator and effect vocabulary.
Correspondences between the theories require independent justification.

Continuation may depend on the particular step, its states, and relevant
context or history.
An operator name alone need not determine the judgment.

## Classification and Profiles

A classification records applicability and identity-persistence behavior
relative to a specified identity criterion and transformation model.

The original classification matrix contains stipulated assignments.
The newer invariant-based construction derives verdicts from declared
identity bases, admissible transformation steps, and their effects.

Derived verdicts distinguish inapplicable (NA), breaking (BRK),
mixed (MIX), preserving (PRS), and neutral (NEU) behavior.
MIX indicates that a transformation family requires refinement
before receiving a uniform classification.

A profile summarizes those judgments.
Different profiles do not automatically establish
different induced identity relations;
that requires an appropriate separation result.

## Limits

Catalog membership, classification differences, and
formally derived verdicts do not independently justify an identity criterion.

Formal derivations establish consequences of specified models and
assumptions.
Scientific adequacy additionally requires
justification of the intended referents, identity criteria, and
transformation warrants.

The selected profiles do not establish a necessary or complete taxonomy.
