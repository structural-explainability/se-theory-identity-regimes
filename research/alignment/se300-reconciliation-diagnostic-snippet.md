## Part A. New section

## 15. N/A to IGN against the paper's profile model

### Status

**The paper's formal profile model keeps N/A (inapplicable) and IGN (applicable
and identity-ignoring) as different things. The Lean total matrix merges them
into IGN. The merge preserves everything the current proofs and theorems use. It
does not preserve the paper's applicability component, and the Lean statement
that the two are "semantically equivalent" is not supported by either the paper
or the one downstream consumer I read.**

### What the paper defines

Source: NR §3.4 to §3.9.

| Definition | Content |
| --- | --- |
| 3.7 | `Appl_P : F → {0,1}`; `Appl_P(f) = 1` iff `f` is applicable under profile `P`. |
| 3.8 | `Class_P` is defined only on `{f : Appl_P(f) = 1}` with values in `{IGN, PRS, BRK}`. For `Appl_P(f) = 0` classification is undefined. In tables such entries are marked N/A, "indicating inapplicability rather than an unclassified applicable transformation". |
| 3.9 | `Persist_P ⊆ {f : Appl_P(f) = 1 and Class_P(f) = PRS}`. |
| 3.10 | A regime profile is the tuple `(C_P, B_P, Appl_P, Class_P, Persist_P)`: carrier, identity basis, applicability, classification, persistence. |
| 3.14 | `~_P` is the equivalence relation generated by the families with `Appl_P(f) = 1` and `Class_P(f) = PRS`. |
| 3.18 to 3.20 | Profile equivalence, collapse and non-collapse are stated on `~_P`. |
| 3.21 to 3.24 | A transformation pattern is a pair `(Appl, Class)`. A profile realizes a pattern iff both components match. Split pressure is stated on the pair `(Appl_P(f), Class_P(f))`. |

### The mapping, stated precisely

Let `π(Appl, Class) = Class` when `Appl = 1`, and `π = IGN` when `Appl = 0`. The
Lean matrix is `π` applied to the paper's pattern at every cell.

### What the mapping preserves and loses, component by component

| Paper component | Under the Lean matrix | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PRS set `{f : Appl = 1, Class = PRS}` | Identical. The eight N/A cells are IGN in Lean and non-PRS in the paper. Lean's 22 PRS cells are all applicable PRS cells. | **Preserved exactly** |
| Induced relation `~_P` (Def 3.14, generated by PRS families only) | Depends only on the PRS set, so it is unchanged. Lean states this in a docstring and does not define `~_P`. | **Preserved**, though not formalized as an object |
| Classification of applicable transformations | 82 of 90 cells carry the same value in both. | **Preserved** |
| Classification at inapplicable transformations | Paper: undefined. Lean: IGN, an invented value at 8 cells. | **Extended** |
| Applicability map `Appl_P` | Not representable. Distinguishable only through comments and the eight `na-to-ign` reference tags. | **Lost** |
| Persistence `Persist_P` | Absent from Lean regardless of the mapping. | **Not modeled** (not caused by the mapping) |
| Carrier `C_P` | Not a profile field. `RegimeVertex.carrier : Ontology` appears only in `Embedding.lean`. | **Not modeled at profile level** |
| Identity basis `B_P` | `IdentityBasis` tag; `.single` shared by OBL, OCC, REC. | **Partial** (see section 13) |
| Patterns, realization, split pressure (Defs 3.21 to 3.24) | `splitTransformation` is a recorded flag, not derived from `(Appl, Class)` pairs. | **Not modeled** |

Of the five components of the paper's profile tuple, Lean represents two in part
(basis as a tag, classification as a total extension) and none of carrier,
applicability, or persistence.

### Non-collapse, assessed separately

Lean defines `NonCollapsing p q := ∃ t, classificationMatrix p t ≠ classificationMatrix q t`
(`Transform/NonCollapse.lean`). The paper's non-collapse is `~_P ≠ ~_Q`
(Def 3.20).

Computed from the committed matrix:

| Profile | PRS set |
| --- | --- |
| OBL | RF, AD |
| OCC | RF, SE |
| REC | AN, RF, AD, BF, PV |
| ENR_L | RF, AD, BF, SE |
| ENR_I | RF, AD, SE |
| CTX_E | RF, AD, RC, BF |
| CTX_S | none |
| NOR_C | RF, AD |
| NOR_S | none |

- **34 of 36 unordered pairs** have a PRS-set difference, so `~_P ≠ ~_Q` follows
  from PRS-generated relations. For 33 of them, at least one PRS-difference cell
  does not touch an `na-to-ign` cell. The 34th (ENR_I, NOR_C) differs in PRS only
  at SE, where NOR_C's cell is N/A in the paper. A PRS versus N/A difference is
  still a PRS difference, so the separation holds under the paper's semantics.
- **Two pairs have equal PRS sets**: (OBL, NOR_C) with `{RF, AD}` and
  (CTX_S, NOR_S) with `{}`. Their only matrix differences are non-PRS cells:
  OBL and NOR_C differ at BF only (IGN versus BRK); CTX_S and NOR_S differ at RC
  and RA only (BRK versus IGN). Neither cell is an `na-to-ign` cell. Under
  `~_P` generated by PRS families alone, these pairs are not separated. The paper
  separates them by carrier (NR §5.6, "GEN-CAR"), which the Lean matrix does not
  model.
- **The Lean header claim** (`Transform/NonCollapse.lean`): "every matrix
  difference that appears in a proof involves a PRS cell for at least one of the
  two profiles being compared, so the Lean definition is operationally equivalent
  to the paper's `~_P ≠ ~_Q`." This claim is false for the two pairs above,
  because their only separating cells are non-PRS. This follows from the matrix
  alone and does not depend on how the tactic proof is elaborated.
- **Tactic replay (not built).** `noncollapse_all_pairs` tries witnesses in the
  order BF, AD, RF, SE, AN, RC, RA, PV, SU. Replaying that order on the committed
  matrix selects a PRS-difference witness for 27 pairs and a non-PRS witness for
  nine (OBL and OCC each against ENR_I, CTX_S, NOR_C and NOR_S at BF, and CTX_S
  against NOR_S at RC). PRS witnesses exist for seven of those nine. Nine selected
  witnesses fall on a cell tagged `na-to-ign` for at least one profile. I did not
  build Lean, so this is a replay of the script's stated order.
- **The N/A to IGN mapping does not cause the gap.** None of the cells
  separating the two equal-PRS pairs is an `na-to-ign` cell.
- **Row distinctness** (`classification_pattern_unique`,
  `representation_theorem`, `derived_regime_set_no_behavioral_collapse`): the nine
  Lean rows are pairwise distinct after the merge. Coarsening can only identify
  rows, so distinctness of the coarser Lean rows implies distinctness of the
  paper's finer `(Appl, Class)` patterns. These Lean results are therefore
  stronger than their paper analogues and are not endangered by the mapping.

### What downstream use requires

- **No theorem in `se-theory-identity-regimes` uses applicability.** The theorems
  that exist (`noncollapse_all_pairs`, `derivedRegimeSet_pairwise_noncollapse`,
  `classification_pattern_unique`, `nine_regime_lower_bound`, the split theorems,
  the embedding results) hold for the coarse matrix, as shown above.
- **One downstream consumer in the series does use it.** AR imports an
  interface with `Class_τ(f) ∈ {PRS, BRK, NEU, NA}` from SE-200
  (`se200.def.TransformationClassification`). Its Check 3, transformation-log
  legality, requires `Class_τ(f_i) ≠ NA` for every logged entry. It states that
  a logged operation classified NA "is an inapplicable operation and will fail
  transformation-log legality", and that an NEU entry "records an applicable
  operation that leaves the declared identity basis unaffected". The same paper
  defines the log's effect values as `{PRS, BRK, NEU}` and says NA "belongs to
  the imported classification function rather than to the effect asserted by a
  successfully applied history entry". SE-210 (arXiv 2607.20729, Def 2.1) uses the
  same four-valued interface.
- **I found no import relationship** between these consumers and the Lean repo or
  its `reference/` artifacts. They consume the SE-200 interface directly. So no
  consumer I examined is fed by the merged matrix.

### Re-evaluation of the Lean statement

"Not applicable is semantically equivalent to identity question does not arise
(IGN)" is not supported:

1. NR Def 3.8 says N/A marks inapplicability, "rather than an unclassified
   applicable transformation". IGN is a value of `Class_P`, which is defined only
   where `Appl_P = 1`.
2. AR Check 3 treats NA and NEU differently.
3. NR itself does not use N/A for "no state evolution in the relevant sense". It
   classifies OBL.SE and REC.SE as IGN with prose saying those entities do not
   undergo state evolution "in the sense relevant to SE" (NR §4.4.1, §4.4.3),
   and marks N/A for OCC.RA and OCC.BF only (§4.4.2). The paper's criterion for
   `Appl = 0` is therefore narrower than "the identity question does not arise".

**Replacement for the Lean statement in the diagnostic:**

> N/A → IGN preserves the PRS-generated identity relations used by the current
> non-collapse machinery, but it does not preserve the paper's independent
> applicability component.

This is supported for `noncollapse_of_prs_difference`, for the three split
theorems, and for 34 of 36 pairs. It does not support the separate Lean claim
that every witness cell is a PRS cell. That claim fails for two pairs for a
reason unrelated to the mapping.

## Part B. New section

## 16. Resolving the SE-300 label

### Question

Is NR the source the Lean calls "SE-300", and what can be established about what
the April and May 2026 comments meant?

### Finding 1: current substantive identification

**NR contains the material that the Lean attributes to "SE-300", with five
qualifications.**

| Lean citation | Where | NR content | Match |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transformation basis, "SE-300 §3" | `Vocab/TransformBasis.lean` | §3.3 Def 3.6 gives `F = {RE, AN, RF, AD, RC, RA, SU, BF, PV, SE}`. §4.2 Def 4.3 gives the ten semantic glosses. | **Verbatim.** The ten Lean comment lines equal Def 4.3 word for word ("restructuring into coarser or finer components", "change in association or bearer", and so on). The Lean cites §3, while the glosses are in §4.2. |
| OBL, OCC, REC rows, "Section 4 canonical classification tables" | `Transform/Core.lean` header and rows | §4.4 gives tables and prose for exactly these three profiles | **Match** under N/A to IGN, including OCC.RA and OCC.BF as `Appl = 0`. Lean's per-cell comments track the NR prose (for example "obligation persists independently of representation forking"). |
| Nine-profile set, "Sections 5.1 to 5.3" | `Transform/Core.lean` header, doc comments | §5.1 to §5.3, Def 5.1 | **Match.** Def 5.1 lists the nine profiles with the descriptors the Lean uses ("enduring referent (locus-bound)", "applicability context (extension-sensitive)", "normative structure (composition-sensitive)", "normative structure (substitution-sensitive)"). |
| ENR split under BF, CTX under AD, NOR under RF; "SE-300 Prop. ENR / CTX / NOR non-collapse" | `Transform/NonCollapse.lean`, `Profile/Core.lean` | Props 6.3, 6.5, 6.7 and §5.2 "Each refinement is forced by split pressure under a specific transformation" | **Match** on transformation. See qualification 2 on direction. |
| Pairwise non-collapse, "SE-300 Section 5" | `Transform/NonCollapse.lean` | §5.6 matrices with entries THM and GEN-CAR | **Match in vocabulary.** The Lean comments use "THM pairs" and "GEN-CAR pairs". Separation routes differ (qualification 4). |
| Lower bound, determinacy, representation | `Transform/LowerBound.lean`, `Embedding.lean` | Thm 5.5 (lower bound of nine), Lemma 5.8 (regime determinacy), Thm 5.9 (representation theorem), Def 5.3 and Thm 5.4 (regime-typed multigraph and faithful embedding) | **Match** in statement and in the names `RegimeVertex`, `RegimeEdge`, `RegimeGraph`. |
| Family vocabulary | `reference/regime-families.toml`, `regime-profiles.toml` | Ex 4.2 and Def 5.1 | **Match.** "Enduring non-normative referent", "normative structure", "time-indexed occurrence", "applicability context", "descriptive record" and the profile labels "obligation-bearing / time-indexed occurrence / descriptive record profile". SE-200's current vocabulary is plain referent, scope and rule instead. |

**Qualifications**

1. **No table for the six split profiles.** NR gives tables only for OBL, OCC and
   REC. The Lean rows for ENR_L, ENR_I, CTX_E, CTX_S, NOR_C and NOR_S, other than
   the split cell, are not in the manuscript. They equal SE-200 Table 1. The Lean
   changelog's "rows verified against SE-300 split sections" cannot be checked
   against NR beyond the split cell. Six of the eight N/A cells (ENR RA and the
   SE cells of CTX and NOR) likewise match SE-200 and not NR.
2. **Split direction.** NR Def 6.4 reads "ENR-L: identity bound to locus
   (branching breaks identity); ENR-I: identity bound to instrument (branching
   preserves identity across instances)". That is the reverse of the Lean
   (ENR_L BF = PRS, ENR_I BF = BRK), of SE-200 (LOC BF = PRS, OBJ BF = BRK), and
   of NR's own §7.1 example ("multiple continuations may refer to the same
   location, but distinct artifacts cannot remain identical"). The Lean follows
   §7.1. This is an internal inconsistency in the manuscript.
3. **NOR labels.** NR §5.3 and Def 6.8 call the profiles "composition-sensitive"
   (NOR-C) and "substitution-sensitive" (NOR-S). NR §7.1 calls them
   "content-sensitive (NOR-C)" and "structure-sensitive (NOR-S)", and describes
   content-tracking and structure-tracking bases. The Lean carries both wordings:
   the doc comments use "composition-sensitive (content-preserving)" and
   "substitution-sensitive (structural)", while the bases are `.content` and
   `.structure`.
4. **Cross-family separation.** NR §5.6 separates profiles with different
   carriers by carrier ("GEN-CAR") and says no separation by basis is needed.
   Lean separates them by matrix cells (`noncollapse_of_distinct_regime`). NR
   Def 4.1 also says regime assignment "depends solely on transformation
   behavior, not carrier category", which sits in tension with GEN-CAR
   separation within the manuscript.
5. **"Sections 5.1 to 5.3 regime refinement definitions"** in the Lean header
   corresponds to the descriptor definitions in §5.3 and the refinement
   definitions in §6.2 to §6.4 (Defs 6.4, 6.6, 6.8).

**Resolved by this identification**

- The origin of "composition-sensitive" and "substitution-sensitive" in the Lean
  comments (an open item since section 8) is NR §5.3 and Def 6.8. It is not a
  Lean-side invention. SU is BRK for every profile in the matrix, so the NR label
  does not discriminate NOR-S. That is a question about the manuscript's label.
- The Lean `TransformBasis` comments are NR Def 4.3 verbatim. The
  `reference/regime-transformations.toml` descriptions match neither NR Def 4.3
  nor SE-200's wording (`paper-200-identity-regimes`,
  `sections/03_formal_setting.tex`, for example RF as "increase in descriptive,
  structural, or normative detail"). They remain unsupported by any source I
  read, which strengthens the draft-residue finding in section 13.
- The strings `event-bound`, `scope-bound` and `context-bound` occur in neither
  NR nor AE6 (exact-string search of the extracted text). The stale labels in
  sections 6 to 9 have no support in SE-300.

### Finding 2: historical provenance

**The evidence does not establish what the April 28 and May 2, 2026 comments
meant by "SE-300", and it does not establish that NR existed in its September 29
form then.**

Evidence consistent with NR (or an earlier draft with the same outline):

- The Lean's April and May comments cite section numbers that agree with NR's
  numbering: §3 for the basis, "Section 4" for canonical tables, "Section 5" for
  lower bound, pairwise matrices and determinacy, and "Sections 5.1 to 5.3" for
  refinement definitions. These agree with NR §3.3, §4.4, §5.5, §5.6, §5.9 and
  §5.1 to §5.3.
- The Lean README at `053104d` (2026-05-02) and `68b223a` (2026-07-25) listed
  "Path grammar and expressive adequacy (SE-300)" among things the repo does not
  own. NR's abstract and §4 are about a path grammar and expressive adequacy. The
  `(SE-300)` suffix was removed in the 2026-09-30 README rewrite (`1d08827`).
- The 0.1.0 changelog (2026-04-28) records matrix decisions "in SE-300 Section 4":
  OCC/AD is BRK, OBL/BF is IGN, REC/BF is PRS. NR §4.4 states those three
  decisions.
- The nine-profile derivation with the names ENR_L, ENR_I, CTX_E and so on was in
  the Lean on 2026-04-28. At that time the SE-200 repo held a six-regime paper
  (`se200_identity_regimes.tex`, state at `25eb3eb`, 2026-05-02), and the
  nine-regime sections of SE-200 first appear on 2026-07-08. The April nine-profile
  source was therefore not SE-200. NR carries this derivation.

Evidence against treating NR as the same document in April:

- The PDF is dated September 29, 2026. It contains unresolved cross-references
  ("established in ?", "Proposition ??") and anonymized citations ("Anonymous
  (2026a, b)"), which marks it as a manuscript in progress.
- Neither NR nor AE6 contains the string "SE-300" or "se300".
- NR has no table for the six split profiles, which the April changelog says were
  "verified against SE-300 split sections".

The section-number agreement is evidence about the outline the Lean author was
reading. It is not evidence about NR's September content.

### Finding 3: the accountable-records source (AR)

**AR is a downstream consumer, not an identity-regime source.**

- Title: "Accountable Records: Conformance Checking for the Neutral-Substrate
  Constraint". It specifies a deployed accountable-record artifact and five
  conformance checks.
- Its abstract names the imported nine regimes as OBL, OCC, REC, LOC, OBJ,
  SCOPE-E, SCOPE-S, RULE-C and RULE-S, which is the SE-200 vocabulary. It does not
  use ENR_L, CTX_E or NOR_C.
- It imports results with stable labels: 6 distinct `se100.*` labels from
  *Neutral Substrates* (`se100.def.NeutralityByDesign`,
  `se100.constraint.Neutrality`, and others) and 11 distinct `se200.*` labels from
  *Referential Regimes* (`se200.def.IdentityRegime`,
  `se200.def.TransformationClassification` with four values,
  `se200.thm.NineRegimeLowerBound`, `se200.cor.Monotonicity`,
  `se200.def.CoreCarrierBasisPair`, and others). It defines no `se300.*` labels.
- It does not use NR's terms "path grammar", "regime-typed", `Appl` or GEN-CAR
  (exact-string search of the source). It does refer to "split axes", which it
  states are imported from *Referential Regimes*.

AR therefore cannot be the source of the Lean's nine-profile derivation or of the
ENR_L and ENR_I naming. It is relevant to this note as the consumer in section 15
that needs applicability.

### Numbering evidence relevant to the ambiguity

- The three documents supplied for this pass carry the same filename prefix:
  `se300_neutral_representation.pdf`, `se300_ae_reference_realization.pdf`,
  `se300-accountable-records.tex`. The prefix therefore does not identify one
  document.
- Of the three, only NR contains the content the Lean cites. AE6 states it
  introduces no new results and gives no tables. AR has no regime derivation.
- The org uses repo numbers `paper-200` (SE-200) and `paper-210`, `paper-320`
  elsewhere. I probed several plausible `paper-3xx` repo names with `git ls-remote`
  and none responded. That is inconclusive, since the repo may be private or
  named differently. I did not locate a repository for NR.

### Conclusion on SE-300

- **Current substantive source: established**, with the qualifications above.
  NR is the one supplied document that contains what the Lean cites.
- **Historical meaning of the April and May comments: not established.**
  Candidate readings are an earlier draft of NR, or a series label (the `se300`
  prefix covers three documents). The evidence cannot choose between them.

## Part C. New section

## 17. The six-kind AE reference realization

Source: AE6, and `spec-ae` history (`git log` on the clone).

### What AE6 says

- It fixes six named kinds: Actor, Locus, Instrument, Event, Scope, Observation,
  for the six regimes required by "Paper 2", citing arXiv 2601.16152. Its Table 1
  maps requirements R1 to R6: R2 to Actor, R3 to Instrument, R4 to Event (with
  Locus for place), R5 to Scope, R6 to Observation, and Locus as "place stability
  implicit in R4/R5". R1 is a global constraint.
- It says it "introduces no new theoretical results", that its realization "is
  not proposed as unique", that Paper 2's claim is that six regimes are necessary
  and sufficient, and that the living specification is
  `https://github.com/structural-explainability/spec-ae`.
- Its §6 says updates to the specification do not modify the article's
  definitions, and "any future revision to the reference definitions would
  require a new published reference artifact".

### Does it explain the six-kind statement in `spec-ae`?

**Yes, historically.**

- `spec-ae` initial commit `6775b79` (2025-12-30): `AE.KIND.EXACT_COUNT.V1` read
  "AE v1 defines exactly six accountable entity kinds." The six kinds were
  unnamed placeholders `AE.KIND.K1` to `AE.KIND.K6`, each required to have a
  regime "distinct from the regimes of all other kinds".
- At that time the SE-200 line (arXiv 2601.16152, and the repo paper of
  2026-05-02 that I read) held the six-regime result with requirements R1 to R6
  and the regime list OBL, NOR, OCC, CTX, REC, ENR. The six in AE v1 corresponds
  to those six.
- `b1151fa` (2026-05-15, "prep 0.9.1") replaced K1 to K6 with nine
  `AE.KIND.PER.*` identifiers and changed `SPEC.md` to nine. It edited
  `README.md` but left the bullet "exactly six entity kinds are defined"
  (README line 39). That bullet is residue from 0.9.0.

### Layers kept separate

| Layer | Content | Source |
