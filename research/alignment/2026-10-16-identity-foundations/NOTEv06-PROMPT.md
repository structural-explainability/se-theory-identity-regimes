## Non-negotiable Scientific Standard

Distinguish three separate achievements:

1. **Mathematical correctness:** Definitions are consistent and results follow from stated assumptions.
2. **Scientific adequacy:** The definitions represent the intended phenomena, with independently justified assumptions, interpretation conditions, and meaningful counterexamples.
3. **Original theoretical contribution:** The theory establishes substantive claims about individuation, identity, persistence, transformation, or explainability that are not merely assumed.

Correct mathematics is not sufficient scientific evidence.

Do not present a new predicate, equivalence relation, identifier, unity rule, or continuation relation as a scientific explanation unless its meaning and adequacy are independently established.

Unresolved foundational obligations must remain explicitly OPEN.

## Phase I — Source Audit

First, investigate independently documented scientific or operational criteria for individuating one concrete experimental execution.

Find a suitable public, authoritative source that genuinely constrains the identity of individual executions.

Possible domains include laboratory experiments, standardized measurement procedures, scientific instrument runs, or other clearly specified empirical operations.

Do not assume that recording requirements, timestamps, unique identifiers, or provenance systems establish occurrence identity.

Select one specific occurrence kind supported by sufficient authoritative documentation.

A narrow, defensible account is preferable to a broad, speculative one.

If multiple source candidates are considered, briefly explain why the selected one provides the strongest basis for the scientific question.

## Phase II — Theory Construction

Using only the established source findings and clearly identified additional assumptions, construct one candidate scientific account of the selected occurrence kind.

Follow the `theory-construction` protocol.

The candidate must answer:

### 1. Intended Referent

What concrete occurrence is being individuated?

Distinguish the occurrence from:

- Its description.
- Its recorded identifier.
- Its participants.
- Its constituent activities.
- Its representations and provenance records.
- Other occurrences generated by the same procedure.

### 2. Existence

What establishes that the occurrence actually took place?

Distinguish evidence of occurrence from the occurrence itself.

### 3. Individuation and Unity

What makes it one occurrence?

What distinguishes it from:

- A component activity.
- A larger encompassing process.
- A concurrent execution.
- An interrupted execution.
- A resumed execution.
- A repeated execution.

If boundaries depend on an operational convention, identify its authoritative basis.

### 4. Referential Identity

What independently justifies the conclusion that two different presentations identify the same concrete occurrence?

The criterion must not merely define sameness as:

- Equality of identifiers.
- Equality of timestamps.
- Equality of descriptions.
- Equality of provenance records.
- Membership in a stipulated equivalence class.

Such relationships may supply evidence when their adequacy is justified.

### 5. Temporal Semantics

Distinguish:

- The occurrence's temporal extent.
- The evaluation time of a presentation.
- The creation or revision time of records.
- The times of transformations affecting either the occurrence or its representation.

Do not infer that an occurrence continues happening because it is described at a later time.

### 6. Strict Identity and Relatedness

Separate numerical identity from:

- Temporal overlap.
- Proper parthood.
- Causal relatedness.
- Succession.
- Repetition.
- Derivation.
- Similarity.

Do not confuse cross-time co-reference with persistence.

### 7. Formal Consequences

State what genuinely follows from the candidate criteria.

Separate:

- Mathematical consequences.
- Substantive scientific consequences.
- Additional assumptions.
- Conjectures.
- Unresolved obligations.

Do not claim an original theoretical contribution when a result merely restates its premises.

## Phase III — Adversarial Testing

Attempt to falsify the candidate.

At minimum, test:

1. Two executions with identical recorded attributes.
2. One execution described in two independent records.
3. One interrupted and resumed execution.
4. Two repetitions of the same procedure.
5. Two concurrent or overlapping executions.
6. A sub-operation and the complete execution.
7. An execution whose record is corrected later.
8. Two records with the same identifier but conflicting underlying evidence.
9. One occurrence assigned two different identifiers.
10. A failure or aborted execution.

For each test:

- State the expected identity judgment, if grounded.
- Identify what the candidate predicts.
- Explain the scientific basis.
- State whether the case defeats, narrows, or leaves the candidate unresolved.

Add stronger adversarial cases where the source material suggests them.

Distinguish construction cases from independently selected application evidence.

Do not describe cases constructed to repair the candidate as independent validation.
