# Structural Explainability — Scientific Research Continuation

## Research Task

Continue the scientific development of Unified Referential Semantics for Structural Explainability (SE).

**Immediate research question:**

For one independently individuated NIST Disk Chopper Spectrometer (DCS) acquisition episode, what independently justified evidence is sufficient to establish that two separately produced records identify that very same episode, rather than distinct executions with compatible attributes?

The objective is to determine whether a scientifically adequate, non-circular account of **cross-record occurrence identity** can actually be established.

This is a scientific investigation, not a coding or repository-maintenance task.

Do not assume that the proposed identity criterion exists or that it can be demonstrated from available evidence.

## Non-Negotiable Scientific Standard

Distinguish three achievements:

1. **Mathematical correctness:** Definitions are consistent and conclusions follow from stated assumptions.
2. **Scientific adequacy:** The definitions represent the intended phenomena, with independently justified assumptions, interpretation conditions, and meaningful counterexamples.
3. **Original theoretical contribution:** The theory establishes substantive results that do not merely restate its assumptions or reproduce existing scientific rules.

Mathematical correctness alone is insufficient.

Do not accept arbitrary equivalence relations, identifiers, provenance chains, predicates, or formal constructions as scientifically adequate identity criteria without independently establishing their relevance and sufficiency.

The objective is to discover what the evidence actually warrants.

Unresolved foundational obligations must remain explicitly OPEN.

# Phase I — Primary-Source Investigation

Investigate actual NIST DCS measurement runs and the available operational evidence identifying them.

Start with the authoritative sources identified in `NOTEv07.md`.

Look for actual, publicly inspectable records and associated instrument-operation histories, rather than relying exclusively on descriptions of how records are normally created.

Determine whether the available materials include independently generated evidence concerning the same acquisition episode.

Investigate:

- How individual acquisition episodes begin and end.
- How runs differ from constituent acquisition cycles.
- How execution episodes are distinguished from configurations, experiment-level designations, and command sequences.
- What instrument-generated evidence establishes actual acquisition.
- What independently produced records can identify the episode.
- How records are linked to the acquisition history.
- Whether different records obtain their identifiers or associations from one common source.
- What establishes the authenticity and reliability of those associations.
- Whether two distinct runs could satisfy the same recorded attributes.
- How conflicting records or missing evidence affect the identity judgment.

### Source requirements

Use primary scientific and operational sources wherever available.

For every material finding, identify:

- Source and relevant section.
- What the source explicitly establishes.
- What is inferred.
- What additional assumptions are needed.
- What remains unverified.

**Do not mistake separately stored records for independently generated evidence.**

Two records may share one incorrect attribution even if they are created by different software systems or authors.

If actual records, operational histories, or provenance information cannot be inspected, report that limitation.

Do not manufacture an example or substitute hypothetical records for independent validation.

### Source-audit verdict

Use the canonical classifications:

- FOUND
- PARTIAL
- NOT FOUND
- UNDER-SPECIFIED

Distinguish an absent criterion from an unread or inaccessible source.

End the source audit with:

**JUSTIFIED FROM CURRENT SOURCES**

**WOULD REQUIRE NEW THEORY OR INTERFACE**

# Phase II — Candidate Theory Construction

Using the audited source findings, construct or revise **one narrowly scoped candidate account**.

The candidate must distinguish:

### 1. Concrete occurrence

What establishes the actual DCS acquisition episode independently of its records?

### 2. Individuation

What distinguishes one acquisition episode from another, including repetitions, concurrent operations, interruptions, and restarts?

### 3. Denotation

What establishes that a particular presentation identifies a particular operational episode?

### 4. Evidential warrant

What evidence is necessary and, if demonstrable, sufficient to warrant that denotation?

### 5. Co-reference

Under what conditions do two independently produced presentations identify that same concrete episode?

### 6. Competing explanations

Could the same observations arise from two distinct episodes, a misattributed record, or a shared erroneous source?

### 7. Temporal semantics

Keep separate:

- Actual acquisition time.
- Run-control lifecycle.
- Record creation and revision time.
- Evaluation time.
- Transformation time.

A later record does not imply continued occurrence.

### 8. Strict identity and relatedness

Distinguish numerical identity from:

- Shared identifiers.
- Similarity.
- Common experimental configuration.
- Temporal overlap.
- Parthood.
- Causal connection.
- Repetition.
- Derivation.
- Succession.

Do not collapse these relationships.

## Avoid Circularity

Do not define successful co-reference by assuming that two records were produced by the same run unless that association has already been independently established.

Likewise, do not assert that provenance establishes identity unless the provenance-to-occurrence relationship has itself been scientifically justified.

Explicitly identify any residual circularity.

A proposed predicate is not a scientific solution simply because it admits a consistent mathematical interpretation.

# Phase III — Adversarial Testing

Attempt to falsify the candidate.

At minimum, investigate:

1. Two different runs having identical recorded attributes.
2. One run described in independently produced records.
3. Two records containing the same incorrect identifier.
4. Two identifiers referring to the same run.
5. One run with changing accumulated datafile contents.
6. Two runs using the same configuration.
7. A run containing several acquisition cycles.
8. A rejected cycle within an otherwise continuing run.
9. An interrupted and resumed acquisition.
10. A terminated run followed by a restart.
11. A requested run that never actually acquires data.
12. A later correction to a record.
13. Two independent records with contradictory claims.
14. An experiment-level record covering several runs.

For each case, distinguish:

- Scientifically grounded judgment.
- Candidate prediction.
- Supporting evidence.
- Additional assumptions.
- Counterexample or unresolved ambiguity.
- Consequence for the candidate.

Clearly distinguish hypothetical construction cases from independently observed scientific examples.

A case created to test or repair the candidate is not independent validation.

# Phase IV — Comparison With the GLP Investigation

Use `NOTEv07-claude.md` as a contrasting investigation.

Do not attempt to combine the DCS and GLP definitions into one universal identity rule.

Instead, investigate what their differences imply for the developing theory.

In particular:

- Operational acquisition versus regulated-study designation.
- Physical execution versus administrative study continuity.
- Instrument records versus independently authored quality-assurance records.
- Component cycles versus experimental phases.
- Actual termination versus regulatory completion.
- Referential evidence versus authoritative constitution.

Evaluate Claude's proposed provenance, convergence, independent-attestation, and no-defeater conditions.

Determine whether they are necessary, sufficient, both, or neither for the selected DCS case, to the extent that the evidence permits.

Do not assume that a shared specimen, recorded identifier, common cause, or traceability chain establishes numerical identity of the larger occurrence.

Preserve the distinction between a scientifically constituted experimental execution and the regulated study that may encompass it.

The GLP case is a comparison, not a second full investigation.
